# OD Next Document Task Profile v3.1.11

Use for reports, guides, manuals, and long-form documents. Resolve the audience, purpose, sections, sources, and content that must be preserved. Deliver a standalone HTML document with route=document-html and kind=document. Use the product's existing PDF/image export; do not claim to have generated DOCX.

Organize the body with semantic article markup, heading levels, paragraphs, lists, and necessary tables. Aim for a reading width of 65–80 characters, allow wide tables to scroll horizontally, and keep mobile reading usable. Table-of-contents anchors must match the body. Provide @media print rules for pagination, margins, and repeating table headers; avoid fixed heights that clip long text.

Cite user-provided or actually read sources; do not invent data, quotations, or sources. Preserve the confirmed structure and tone. Limit small edits to the relevant sections; one Skill's delivery requirements do not override another's. Deliver the agreed HTML honestly without claiming exports outside the product's capabilities.

## Writing from supplied material

When organizing supplied records or operating rules, write from those facts at
their original level of detail. A fictional subject is still source-bound;
invent additional content only when the user explicitly requests it. Apply this
boundary while outlining and writing every heading, caption, table, and paragraph.

- Keep supplied facts and explicit relationships, including before/after
  sequence. Missing causes or measurements do not erase a stated relationship.
  When no relationship is supplied, keep the facts separate rather than adding
  chronology, shared conditions, physical identity, or causality.
- Describe a source omission as "not provided in the supplied material". Reserve
  "not recorded", "not tested", "absent", and similar historical claims for
  explicit source statements. A summary with means or counts does not establish
  whether individual observations were recorded or what happened outside it.
- Use the supplied operation statements as the steps. Do not expand one action
  into a familiar sequence of component handling, control gestures, or recovery
  behavior. Omit unspecified mechanisms; ask only if the missing rule blocks
  useful delivery. A complete manual covers the provided rules completely.
- Keep calculations traceable to supplied numbers. Label proposed explanations
  or future methods locally as hypotheses or proposals, without adding supposed
  past events to justify them. Preserve fictional, estimated, or provisional
  qualifiers visibly in each requested document.
- Fit length and structure to the supplied content. A continuous document can
  be short; omit empty sections and unsupported background instead of filling
  a conventional report or manual storyline.

## Production rules

- Establish the information hierarchy first: the title names the subject, the opening explains the purpose, and sections follow the reader's workflow or the argument. For manuals, organize the supplied rules into the requested sections; a section name does not authorize missing operating facts. Do not turn the body into a marketing landing page.
- Distinguish facts, user data, assumptions, and examples. Preserve units, dates, and measurement definitions for numbers. Give tables titles, headers, and necessary notes. Do not remove requested details for visual polish.
- Use one main heading and a sequential section hierarchy. Long documents need a working table of contents; charts need captions and sources. Keep body text and tables fully readable on narrow screens rather than simulating the entire document on a fixed canvas.
- Hide navigation controls in print styles. Use break-before / break-inside appropriately to avoid orphaned headings or split table rows. Do not replace selectable text with screenshots. Deliver within existing export capabilities; print styles alone do not prove a verified PDF.
